Al-Farabi

Central Asian philosopher and political theorist who systematized Islamic philosophy and wrote on the ideal state.

Who or what

Abu Nasr Muhammad al-Farabi (c. 872–950), known as the "Second Teacher" (after Aristotle), was a Persian or Central Asian philosopher.

He wrote influential commentaries on Aristotle and Plato and developed an original political philosophy in "The Virtuous City" (Al-Madina al-Fadila).

Why it mattered

Al-Farabi was the first Islamic philosopher to fully articulate a systematic philosophy encompassing logic, epistemology, metaphysics, and political theory.

He envisioned an ideal society led by a philosopher-prophet, anticipating Plato's philosopher-king but within an Islamic framework.

Key works and moments

His "Book of Letters" analyzed the origin of language and philosophy.

He classified the sciences into a hierarchy: linguistics, logic, mathematics, physics, metaphysics, and politics.

Further detail

The "Second Teacher" title reflects his role in explaining and expanding Aristotle's Organon, adding new commentaries on the Prior and Posterior Analytics.

Al-Farabi's political philosophy synthesized Plato's Republic with Islamic concepts of prophecy and revelation.

Legacy

His classification of knowledge influenced the medieval European university curriculum.

His ideas on the virtuous society influenced later political thinkers like Ibn Sina and Ibn Rushd.
