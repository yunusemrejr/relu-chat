Al-Ghazali

Influential Islamic theologian, jurist, and mystic who critiqued excessive rationalism in philosophy.

Who or what

Abu Hamid Muhammad al-Ghazali (1058–1111) was a Persian theologian and Sufi mystic.

His "Incoherence of the Philosophers" critiqued Neoplatonic philosophy's claims to certain knowledge.

Why it mattered

Al-Ghazali did not reject reason itself but argued that philosophical metaphysics overstepped its bounds.

His spiritual crisis and turn to Sufism produced one of the most influential works of Islamic spirituality.

Key works and moments

He identified 20 points where philosophers contradicted Islamic doctrine, particularly on the eternity of the world and God's knowledge of particulars.

His "Revival of the Religious Sciences" synthesized jurisprudence, theology, and mysticism.

Further detail

His critique targeted the Neoplatonic emanation theory and the philosophers' claim of necessary causal connections.

He argued that causation is habitual, not necessary — anticipating Hume's critique of causation by 600 years.

Legacy

His work prompted responses from Ibn Rushd and shaped the boundaries between philosophy and theology.

His spiritual writings remain among the most influential in Islamic thought.

Related topics

  • Ibn Sina (Avicenna)Persian polymath whose "Canon of Medicine" was the standard medical text in Europe and the Islamic world for 600 years.
  • Philosophy in the Golden AgeThe philosophical tradition that synthesized Greek philosophy with Islamic theology.
  • Ibn TufaylAndalusian philosopher and physician whose novel "Hayy ibn Yaqzan" explored the nature of reason and revelation.
