Fatima al-Fihri

Arab woman who founded the University of al-Qarawiyyin, the oldest existing degree-granting university in the world.

Who or what

Fatima bint Muhammad al-Fihri (c. 800–880) was an Arab woman from Kairouan (Tunisia) who migrated to Fez, Morocco, and founded the al-Qarawiyyin Mosque and Madrasa in 859 CE.

The associated institution evolved into the University of al-Qarawiyyin, recognized by UNESCO and the Guinness World Records as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution awarding degrees.

Why it mattered

Fatima used her inheritance from her father to build a mosque and school that would serve her community and become a beacon of learning for centuries.

Her story is a remarkable example of female patronage of education in the 9th century.

Key works and moments

Al-Qarawiyyin attracted scholars from across the Islamic world, including Ibn Rushd (Averroes), Ibn al-Arabi, and the Jewish philosopher Maimonides.

The library of al-Qarawiyyin holds some of the oldest manuscripts in existence, including a 9th-century Quran.

Further detail

The institution began as a mosque-madrasa (school) teaching Quran, law, and grammar, and gradually expanded to include mathematics, astronomy, and medicine.

It was among the first institutions to award formal degrees (ijazas) upon completion of studies.

Legacy

Al-Qarawiyyin educated scholars who shaped Islamic and European intellectual history for over a millennium.

It demonstrates the central role of educational institutions in the Islamic Golden Age.
