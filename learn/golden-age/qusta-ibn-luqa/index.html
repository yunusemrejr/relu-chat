Qusta ibn Luqa

Melkite Christian translator, physician, and philosopher who bridged Greek and Arabic medicine and science.

Who or what

Qusta ibn Luqa al-Ba'albaki (820–912) was a Melkite Christian physician, philosopher, astronomer, and translator from Baalbek (modern Lebanon).

He translated numerous Greek works into Arabic, including the "Mechanics" of Hero of Alexandria and works by Aristotle, Galen, and Diophantus.

Why it mattered

Qusta was one of the few translators who wrote extensively on his own intellectual contributions, not just translations.

He wrote important original treatises on medicine, optics, and the relationship between body and soul.

Key works and moments

His treatise "On the Difference Between the Spirit and the Soul" was the first systematic psychological work in Arabic.

He wrote on medical topics including epidemics, sexual health, and the therapeutic use of music.

Further detail

He translated the pseudo-Aristotelian "Mechanics" (Mechanica Problemata), which influenced Islamic mechanical engineering.

His original works were widely cited by later Islamic physicians and philosophers.

Legacy

His translations preserved important Greek scientific works that might otherwise have been lost.

His philosophical writings influenced the development of Islamic psychology and medicine.
