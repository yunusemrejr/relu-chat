Abbas ibn Firnas

Andalusian polymath who made the first recorded attempt at controlled human flight and invented various devices.

Who or what

Abu'l-Qasim Abbas ibn Firnas (c. 810–887) was an Andalusian polymath from Ronda (near Cordoba), known for early flight attempt, crystal manufacture, and mechanical inventions.

In 875 CE, at age 65, he reportedly glided for several minutes using a silk-covered wooden frame, making him the first person to attempt controlled flight.

Why it mattered

Ibn Firnas is history's first "aviator" — his flight attempt over Cordoba predates Eilmer of Malmesbury's and Leonardo da Vinci's work by 600 years.

He was a true polymath: astronomer, chemist, musician, inventor, and poet.

Key works and moments

He manufactured colorless crystal glass from raw materials, improving on colored glass techniques.

He invented a water clock, an armillary sphere corrected for planetary positions, and a corrective lens for reading.

Further detail

His flying machine was a wooden frame covered with silk and actual bird feathers; witnesses reported he flew for a considerable distance before crashing.

He later identified that the failure was due to the lack of a tail mechanism — an insight into aerodynamic stability.

Legacy

Ibn Firnas is honored as the first scientific aeronaut; his name adorns a crater on the Moon and a bridge in Cordoba.

His contributions to glassmaking impacted the development of optics and scientific instruments.
