Islamic Golden Age

Astrolabe

Sophisticated astronomical instrument for solving problems relating to time, position, and celestial motion.

Who or what

A planar projection instrument consisting of a mater (body), rete (star map), plates (climate-specific projections), and alidade (sighting rule).

Could determine time, latitude, prayer directions, and planetary positions from stellar observations.

Why it mattered

The astrolabe was the smartphone of the medieval world — a portable analog computer for astronomy, navigation, and timekeeping.

Islamic scholars perfected the astrolabe, adding universal plates and spherical projections.

Key works and moments

Al-Zarqali's saphea astrolabe worked at any latitude without changing plates.

Nasir al-Din al-Tusi's astrolabe incorporated his mathematical innovations for more accurate calculations.

Further detail

The stereographic projection from the south pole onto the equatorial plane preserved angles, making the astrolabe mathematically precise.

The shadow square on the back enabled height and distance calculations using trigonometry.

Legacy

Astrolabes enabled navigation across the Indian Ocean and later the Atlantic.

They were essential for determining prayer times and the qibla direction across the Islamic world.
