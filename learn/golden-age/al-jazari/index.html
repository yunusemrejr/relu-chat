Al-Jazari

Greatest mechanical engineer of the medieval Islamic world, known for his automata and programmable machines.

Who or what

Badi' al-Zaman Abu'l-Izz ibn Isma'il al-Jazari (1136–1206) was a Muslim polymath, mechanical engineer, and artisan from the Jazira region (modern-day Upper Mesopotamia).

His "Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices" described 100 mechanical devices with detailed instructions and diagrams.

Why it mattered

Al-Jazari was the da Vinci of the Islamic world — centuries before the European Renaissance, he designed programmable robots, crankshafts, and water pumps.

His book was designed as a practical manual for craftsmen, unlike earlier theoretical works.

Key works and moments

The elephant clock combined an Indian water clock, Egyptian phoenix automaton, Chinese dragons, and Arabic/mechanical elements in a stunning multicultural timepiece.

He designed a hand-washing automaton with a programmable sequence (flush, soap, rinse) — the earliest known programmable humanoid robot.

Further detail

He invented the crankshaft and the connecting rod, crucial components of the internal combustion engine and modern machinery.

His cam-based automata used pegs on a rotating drum to trigger mechanical actions at specific times, a form of mechanical programming.

Legacy

His crank-connecting rod mechanism is fundamental to modern machinery — engines, pumps, and industrial automation.

His automata influenced European clockmaking and mechanical engineering via translations of his work.
