Islamic Golden Age

Translation Movement

Massive project to translate Greek, Persian, Indian, and Syriac scientific works into Arabic.

Who or what

Beginning in the 8th century under the Abbasids, scholars translated the scientific heritage of antiquity into Arabic.

The movement was state-sponsored, with caliphs paying translators their weight in gold.

Why it mattered

It was the largest knowledge-transfer project in human history — preserving works that would have been lost and expanding them.

Without this movement, much of Greek science and philosophy would have been lost to humanity.

Key works and moments

Hunayn ibn Ishaq alone translated nearly all of Galen's works, plus Aristotle, Plato, and Hippocrates.

Indian astronomical tables (the Sindhind) and the decimal numeral system were translated and adopted.

Further detail

Translation was systematic: teams of scholars fluent in Greek/Syriac and Arabic worked together for accuracy.

Translators developed Arabic scientific terminology from scratch — words like "algebra," "algorithm," and "alkali" entered via this process.

Legacy

The translated works became the foundation for original Islamic scholarship, which later flowed back to Europe via Toledo and Sicily.

The translation movement established Arabic as the lingua franca of science for 500 years.
