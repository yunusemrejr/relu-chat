Islamic Golden Age

Development of Algebra

The evolution of algebra from Al-Khwarizmi's rhetorical algebra to symbolic notation.

Who or what

Algebra evolved through three stages: rhetorical (words only), syncopated (abbreviations), and symbolic (modern notation).

Islamic scholars carried algebra from geometric problem-solving to a general theory of equations.

Why it mattered

Al-Khwarizmi's algebra was rhetorical — equations written entirely in words. "A square and ten roots equal thirty-nine."

Omar Khayyam classified and solved cubic equations geometrically; al-Karaji began arithmetizing algebra.

Key works and moments

Thabit ibn Qurra gave a general proof for amicable numbers using algebraic reasoning.

Al-Samawal's work on polynomial division anticipated modern algebra by centuries.

Further detail

The six canonical forms of Al-Khwarizmi were gradually reduced as negative numbers gained acceptance.

The fundamental theorem of algebra (every polynomial has a root) was conjectured but not proven until Gauss.

Legacy

Algebra became the language of all quantitative science.

The abstraction from specific numbers to general symbols enabled modern mathematics and physics.
