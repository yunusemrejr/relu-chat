Islamic Golden Age

Scholars, institutions and ideas of the Islamic Golden Age: algebra, optics, medicine and philosophy.

A

  • Abbas ibn FirnasAndalusian polymath who made the first recorded attempt at controlled human flight and invented various devices.
  • Al-BattaniArab astronomer and mathematician who made remarkably accurate astronomical observations and improved Ptolemy's star catalog.
  • Al-BiruniPersian scholar who made groundbreaking contributions to astronomy, geography, and comparative religion.
  • Al-FarabiCentral Asian philosopher and political theorist who systematized Islamic philosophy and wrote on the ideal state.
  • Al-FarghaniCentral Asian astronomer whose summary of Ptolemaic astronomy became a standard textbook in Europe and the Islamic world.
  • Al-GhazaliInfluential Islamic theologian, jurist, and mystic who critiqued excessive rationalism in philosophy.
  • Al-IdrisiArab geographer and cartographer who created the most accurate world map and geographical encyclopedia of the pre-modern era.
  • Al-JazariGreatest mechanical engineer of the medieval Islamic world, known for his automata and programmable machines.
  • Al-KarajiPersian mathematician who freed algebra from geometry by arithmetizing algebraic operations.
  • Al-KhwarizmiPersian mathematician, astronomer, and geographer whose influential works systematized algebra and described arithmetic methods.
  • Al-KindiFirst major Islamic philosopher who synthesized Greek philosophy with Islamic thought.
  • Al-MajritiAndalusian mathematician, astronomer, and alchemist who established science in Islamic Spain.
  • Al-Razi (Rhazes)Persian physician and alchemist who distinguished smallpox from measles and pioneered clinical observation.
  • Al-SamawalArab mathematician who developed techniques for polynomial arithmetic and division.
  • Al-Zahrawi (Abulcasis)Andalusian surgeon whose medical encyclopedia was the standard surgical text in Europe for 500 years.
  • Al-ZarqaliAndalusian astronomer and instrument maker who invented the universal astrolabe and improved astronomical tables.
  • AlgebraMathematical discipline founded by Al-Khwarizmi for solving equations through systematic transformation.
  • AstrolabeSophisticated astronomical instrument for solving problems relating to time, position, and celestial motion.
  • Astronomy in the Golden AgeHighly advanced astronomical science developed in the Islamic world, combining Greek, Persian, and Indian traditions.

B

  • Banu Musa BrothersThree Persian brothers who produced the "Book of Ingenious Devices," a major work on mechanical engineering and automata.

C

  • Canon of MedicineIbn Sina's medical encyclopedia that systematized all known medical knowledge and remained authoritative for six centuries.

D

  • Development of AlgebraThe evolution of algebra from Al-Khwarizmi's rhetorical algebra to symbolic notation.

F

  • Fatima al-FihriArab woman who founded the University of al-Qarawiyyin, the oldest existing degree-granting university in the world.

H

  • House of WisdomMajor intellectual center in Abbasid Baghdad that housed a vast library and translation institute.
  • Hunayn ibn IshaqNestorian Christian translator, physician, and scholar who led the translation of Greek medical texts into Arabic.

I

  • Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)Arab scientist who founded optics as an experimental science and pioneered the scientific method.
  • Ibn al-NafisArab physician who discovered pulmonary circulation 300 years before William Harvey.
  • Ibn al-ShatirDamascene astronomer whose lunar and planetary models were mathematically identical to Copernicus' later formulations.
  • Ibn Bajjah (Avempace)Andalusian philosopher, astronomer, and physician who developed a naturalistic philosophy and anticipated Galilean motion.
  • Ibn KhaldunArab historian, philosopher, and social scientist whose Muqaddimah laid foundations for modern historiography and sociology.
  • Ibn Rushd (Averroes)Andalusian philosopher and jurist whose commentaries on Aristotle shaped medieval European thought.
  • Ibn SahlPersian mathematician and optician who discovered the law of refraction (Snell's law) 600 years before Snell.
  • Ibn Sina (Avicenna)Persian polymath whose "Canon of Medicine" was the standard medical text in Europe and the Islamic world for 600 years.
  • Ibn TufaylAndalusian philosopher and physician whose novel "Hayy ibn Yaqzan" explored the nature of reason and revelation.

L

  • Lubana of CordobaAndalusian female mathematician and librarian who served at the Library of Cordoba.

M

  • Maragha SchoolSchool of astronomers at the Maragha Observatory who developed alternatives to Ptolemaic astronomy.
  • Mariam al-Astrulabi10th-century Syrian female astronomer and astrolabe maker, one of the few known women scientists of the Golden Age.
  • Medicine in the Golden AgeMedical science as practiced and advanced in the Islamic world, combining Greek theory with clinical observation.

  • Nasir al-Din al-TusiPersian polymath who founded the Maragha Observatory and developed the Tusi couple, influencing Copernican astronomy.
