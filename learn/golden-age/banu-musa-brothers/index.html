Banu Musa Brothers

Three Persian brothers who produced the "Book of Ingenious Devices," a major work on mechanical engineering and automata.

Who or what

Muhammad, Ahmad, and al-Hasan ibn Musa ibn Shakir (9th century) were three Persian brothers who studied at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad.

Their "Book of Ingenious Devices" (Kitab al-Hiyal) described 100 mechanical devices including trick vessels, fountains, and automata.

Why it mattered

The Banu Musa were among the first to apply Greek theoretical mechanics to practical engineering problems.

Their automatic fountain with intermittent flow used a siphonic principle that was a precursor to modern feedback control.

Key works and moments

Their descriptions of valves and trick vessels illustrate how fluid flow can be controlled mechanically.

Their fountain designs used water flow and air pressure to produce changing effects.

Further detail

Their work included sophisticated float-valve control systems, differential pressure sensors, and self-trimming lamps.

The Book of Ingenious Devices is distinct from al-Jazari's later work in its focus on Greek-inspired theoretical mechanics over practical craft.

Legacy

Their self-regulating mechanisms and feedback controls anticipated modern automation and control theory.

Their engineering methods influenced both Islamic and European technological development.
