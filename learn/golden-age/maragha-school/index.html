Islamic Golden Age

Maragha School

School of astronomers at the Maragha Observatory who developed alternatives to Ptolemaic astronomy.

Who or what

Founded by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi in 1259 in northwestern Persia, the Maragha school produced revolutionary astronomical models.

The observatory housed a library of 400,000 volumes and instruments including a 6-meter sextant.

Why it mattered

The Tusi couple — two circles where one rolls inside the other — generated linear motion from circular motion.

This resolved a key problem in Ptolemy's models and influenced Copernicus directly.

Key works and moments

Al-Tusi's planetary models eliminated the equant point that violated uniform circular motion.

Ibn al-Shatir's models for the Moon and planets were mathematically identical to Copernicus' later models.

Further detail

The Maragha school's innovations: universal solutions replacing ad-hoc Ptolemaic devices, physical reality as criterion, mathematical consistency.

Copernicus likely encountered these ideas through Byzantine Greek translations circulating in Italy.

Legacy

The Maragha school bridged Islamic and European astronomy — their models directly influenced the Copernican revolution.

The observatory model influenced later institutions like Ulugh Beg's Samarkand observatory and Tycho Brahe's Uraniborg.
