K-Means Clustering

K-means partitions nnn points into kkk clusters, each represented by its centroid, minimizing within-cluster variance.

Ask the Data science assistant1 min read · Updated September 9, 2026

Definition

Algorithm: (1) initialize kkk centroids, (2) assign each point to nearest centroid, (3) update centroids as the mean of assigned points, (4) repeat until convergence.

Converges to a local minimum — different initializations can yield different results.

Intuition

The centroid is the "representative point" of a cluster; new points are assigned to the cluster of their nearest centroid.

K-means assumes clusters are spherical and evenly sized — it struggles with elongated or unequally-sized clusters.

Worked example

With k=3k=3k=3 on customer data, you might discover clusters corresponding to "budget shoppers", "regulars", and "high spenders".

Compressing colors in an image: cluster pixel colors into kkk palette values, then replace each pixel with its centroid.

Elbow method: plot WCSS vs kkk; the "elbow" where adding more clusters gives diminishing returns suggests a good kkk.

In practice

Used for market segmentation, image quantization, and as a preprocessing step before supervised learning.

For high-dimensional data, consider PCA first to reduce dimensionality and speed up clustering.
