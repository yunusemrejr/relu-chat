Probability Basics

Probability quantifies uncertainty, measuring how likely an event is on a scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).

Definition

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B | A) for dependent events.

Bayes' theorem: P(A∣B)=P(B∣A)⋅P(A)P(B)P(A | B) = \frac{P(B | A) \cdot P(A)}{P(B)}P(A∣B)=P(B)P(B∣A)⋅P(A)​, updating belief in A after observing B.

Intuition

Conditional probability P(A∣B)P(A | B)P(A∣B) is the probability of A given that B has occurred — it restricts the sample space.

The "base rate fallacy" ignores P(A)P(A)P(A): a rare event with high false-positive rate often has low true positive predictive value.

Worked example

If 1% of people have a disease and the test is 99% accurate, P(positive∣sick)=0.99P(\text{positive} | \text{sick}) = 0.99P(positive∣sick)=0.99 but P(sick∣positive)≈50%P(\text{sick} | \text{positive}) \approx 50\%P(sick∣positive)≈50% due to the low base rate.

Drawing two aces without replacement from a deck: P(2nd ace∣1st ace)=3/51P(\text{2nd ace} | \text{1st ace}) = 3/51P(2nd ace∣1st ace)=3/51.

The math

Random variable XXX maps outcomes to numbers; the PMF P(X=x)P(X = x)P(X=x) gives probabilities for discrete XXX, the PDF f(x)f(x)f(x) for continuous XXX.

Expectation E[X]=∑x⋅P(X=x)E[X] = \sum x \cdot P(X = x)E[X]=∑x⋅P(X=x); variance Var(X)=E[(X−E[X])2]\text{Var}(X) = E[(X - E[X])^2]Var(X)=E[(X−E[X])2].

In practice

Powers Bayes' theorem for spam filtering, medical diagnosis, and A/B testing — everywhere you update beliefs with evidence.

Probability distributions model real phenomena: binomial for coin flips, Poisson for rare events per time interval, normal for measurement errors.
