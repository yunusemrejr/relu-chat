Probability Distributions

A probability distribution assigns probabilities to outcomes of a random variable; key distributions model common data-generating processes.

Intuition

The central limit theorem says the sum/mean of i.i.d. random variables converges to normal as nnn grows — this is why normal appears everywhere.

Poisson models "events per interval" — arrivals per minute, defects per item — with a single rate parameter λ\lambdaλ.

Worked example

Heights of adult humans approximate N(170,92)N(170, 9^2)N(170,92) cm; IQ is centered at 100 with std 15.

Number of spam emails in an hour might follow Poisson(7) if you average 7 per hour.

The math

Normal properties: 68% of mass within 1σ1\sigma1σ, 95% within 2σ2\sigma2σ, 99.7% within 3σ3\sigma3σ of μ\muμ.

Standard normal Z=(X−μ)/σZ = (X - \mu)/\sigmaZ=(X−μ)/σ; used to compute normal probabilities via ZZZ-tables or scipy.stats.norm.cdf.

In practice

Assume normal errors in linear regression; check residuals visually to validate.

Use Poisson regression for count data; binomial for binary outcomes.
