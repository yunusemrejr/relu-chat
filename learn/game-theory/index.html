A

  • Algorithmic Mechanism DesignThe interface of mechanism design and computer science, concerned with computational tractability of incentive-compatible outcomes.
  • Auction TheoryStudy of resource allocation via bidding mechanisms.

B

  • Backward InductionAlgorithm for solving finite perfect-information games by reasoning from terminal nodes back to the root.
  • Banzhaf Power IndexA measure of a voter's ability to influence the outcome of a vote, calculated as the number of coalitions in which they are pivotal.
  • Battle of the SexesA coordination game with asymmetric preferences over the two coordinated outcomes.
  • Bayesian GameA game in which players have private information (types) drawn from a common prior.
  • Berge EquilibriumAn equilibrium where each player maximizes the payoff of all others, assuming they do not deviate from their Berge strategies.
  • Bertrand CompetitionSimultaneous price-setting competition among firms producing a homogeneous good.
  • Best-response tableA best-response table marks the actions that maximize each player’s payoff against each fixed action of the opponent. Mutual best…

C

  • Centipede GameA multi-stage game where backward induction predicts immediate termination despite cooperative gains.
  • Cheap TalkCostless, non-binding, and verifiable communication prior to play that can influence equilibrium selection.
  • Chicken GameAn anti-coordination game where both players prefer to yield if the other stays the course.
  • Coalition FormationStudy of which coalitions form and how they share surplus.
  • Common KnowledgeAn event is common knowledge if everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on ad infinitum.
  • Congestion GamesGames where each player's payoff depends on the load each facility carries.
  • Cooperative GameA game where binding agreements among coalitions are allowed; analysis focuses on joint payoffs.
  • Core (of a Cooperative Game)The set of coalition-stable payoff allocations.
  • Core Selection (Matching)The selection of an allocation from the core of a matching market, typically via deferred-acceptance algorithms.
  • Correlated EquilibriumAn equilibrium in which players obey recommendations from a common randomization device.
  • Cournot CompetitionA simultaneous-move duopoly/oligopoly with quantity as the strategic variable.

D

  • Dominant StrategyA strategy that yields at least as high a payoff as any alternative regardless of others' play.

E

  • Evolutionarily Stable SetsA set of strategies such that every strategy in the set resists invasion by any outside mutant.
  • Evolutionarily Stable StrategyA strategy that, if adopted by the population, cannot be invaded by any rare mutant.
  • Evolutionary Game TheoryStudies strategy frequencies in large populations under selection and mutation.
  • Extensive-Form GameA representation of sequential play using a rooted tree with information sets.

F

  • Fair DivisionThe study of how to divide resources among agents so that each receives a fair share according to some criterion.
  • Fictitious PlayA learning process in which players best-respond to the empirical distribution of past opponent play.
  • Folk TheoremAny individually rational feasible payoff can be sustained in an infinitely repeated game with patient enough players.

G

  • Global GamesGames of incomplete information where players observe noisy signals of an underlying state, often producing unique equilibrium.

H

  • Harsanyi TransformationTransforms games of incomplete information into games of imperfect information via a type space.
  • Hawk-Dove GameA symmetric 2x2 conflict game used to model animal contests.

I

  • Iterated Elimination of Dominated StrategiesIteratively removing strategies that are strictly dominated.

L

  • Level-k ReasoningA bounded-rationality model where players best-respond to lower-level opponents in a hierarchy of strategic depth.
  • Limits of equilibrium predictionsAn equilibrium is a consistency condition on strategies and beliefs under a specified model. It does not guarantee that people will…

M

  • Matching PenniesA zero-sum 2 2 game with no pure equilibrium and a unique uniform mixed equilibrium.
  • Mean Field GameA game with a continuum of players where each player's influence on aggregate is negligible, simplifying equilibrium to a PDE system.
  • Mechanism DesignDesigning the rules of a game so that equilibrium behavior yields desired outcomes.
  • Minimax TheoremIn any finite two-player zero-sum game, max-min and min-max values coincide in mixed strategies.
  • Mixed StrategyA probability distribution over a player's pure strategies.
  • Mixed-equilibrium calculationTo find an interior mixed equilibrium, choose each player’s mixing probability to make the other player indifferent between actions in…

N

  • Nash Bargaining SolutionThe unique allocation satisfying Pareto-efficiency, symmetry, scale invariance, and IIA.
  • Nash EquilibriumA profile of strategies where no player can profit by unilateral deviation.
  • No-Regret LearningLearning dynamics where a player's cumulative payoff is no worse than the best fixed action in hindsight, up to a vanishing average error.
  • Non-Cooperative GameA game in which binding agreements are not possible; each player chooses independently.
  • Normal-Form GameA simultaneous-move representation via strategy sets and payoff functions.

P

  • Pareto EfficiencyAn allocation is Pareto efficient if no player can be made strictly better off without making someone else worse off.
  • Perfect Bayesian EquilibriumA refinement for dynamic games of incomplete information pairing strategies with beliefs.
  • Potential GamesGames where all unilateral payoff changes are captured by a single real-valued function.
  • Price of AnarchyThe worst-case ratio between the social cost of a Nash equilibrium and the social optimum.
  • Price of StabilityThe best-case ratio between the social cost of a Nash equilibrium and the social optimum.
  • Principal-Agent ProblemA contracting problem where an agent's action is unobservable to the principal.
  • Prisoner's DilemmaCanonical 2x2 game where individually rational defection leads to a Pareto-inferior outcome.
  • Proper EquilibriumA refinement of trembling-hand perfection where more costly mistakes are infinitely less likely.
  • Public-Goods GameA game in which individuals contribute to a shared good; the efficient outcome is not individually rational.
  • Pure StrategyA deterministic choice of an action from a player's strategy set.

Q

  • Quantal Response EquilibriumAn equilibrium where players best-respond noisily with choice probabilities increasing in expected payoff.

R

  • RationalizabilityThe set of strategies surviving iterated elimination of strategies that are never a best response.
  • Repeated GameA stage game played repeatedly, allowing histories to influence future play.
  • Replicator DynamicsODEs describing how strategy frequencies evolve under selection proportional to fitness.
  • Revelation PrincipleAny equilibrium outcome of any mechanism can be reproduced by a direct truthful mechanism.
  • Rubinstein BargainingA non-cooperative alternating-offers model of bargaining with a unique subgame-perfect outcome.

S

  • Screening GamesGames where an uninformed principal offers a menu of contracts to sort informed agents.
  • Sequential EquilibriumA refinement of PBE requiring beliefs to be limits of fully mixed strategy perturbations.
  • Shapley ValueA unique fair allocation in cooperative games satisfying efficiency, symmetry, dummy, and additivity.
  • Signaling GamesDynamic incomplete-information games where an informed sender chooses a signal that a receiver observes.
  • Smoothed Analysis of Price of AnarchyA framework showing that worst-case PoA bounds can be overly pessimistic — typical-case bounds are often dramatically better.
  • Social Choice TheoryThe theoretical study of how individual preferences and judgments can be aggregated into collective decisions.
  • Stackelberg CompetitionA sequential duopoly in which a leader commits to an output and the follower best-responds.
  • Stag HuntA coordination game where mutual cooperation on the risky action maximizes welfare.
  • Subgame Perfect EquilibriumA Nash equilibrium that also prescribes a Nash equilibrium in every subgame.
  • Supermodular GamesA game in which a player's incentive to raise their action increases with opponents' actions (strategic complements).

T

  • Tit-for-TatA simple reciprocal strategy — cooperate first, then mimic the opponent's previous action.
  • Tragedy of the CommonsOveruse of a shared resource when individual users ignore the externality they impose on others.
  • Trembling-Hand PerfectionAn equilibrium robust to small probabilities of mistakes.

U

  • Ultimatum GameA two-stage bargaining game highlighting the gap between rational prediction and observed fairness.

V

  • Vickrey AuctionA sealed-bid auction where the highest bidder wins and pays the second-highest bid.
  • Voting TheoryThe study of procedures for aggregating individual preferences into a collective decision.

W

  • Wardrop EquilibriumA flow assignment in which every used path has equal cost and no unused path has lower cost.

Z

  • Zermelo's TheoremIn finite two-player zero-sum games of perfect information without chance and draws allowed, either one player has a winning strategy or…
  • Zero-Sum GameA game in which one player's gain exactly offsets the others' losses.
