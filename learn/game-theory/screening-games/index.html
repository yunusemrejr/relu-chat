Screening Games

Games where an uninformed principal offers a menu of contracts to sort informed agents.

Definition

Principal offers menu {(qθ,tθ)}θ∈Θ\{(q_\theta,t_\theta)\}_{\theta\in\Theta}{(qθ​,tθ​)}θ∈Θ​ satisfying IC (θ\thetaθ prefers (qθ,tθ)(q_\theta,t_\theta)(qθ​,tθ​) to any other item) and IR.

In Rothschild-Stiglitz insurance screening, high-risk types take full insurance and low-risk types accept partial coverage.

Intuition

The principal designs offers so that each type self-selects into a contract that reveals its type.

Incentive-compatibility and participation constraints shape the menu.

Worked example

Airline fare classes (fully refundable vs. non-refundable) screen business vs. leisure travelers.

Quantity discounts screen heavy vs. light users.

The math

Taxation principle: any incentive-compatible allocation can be implemented through a price schedule, reducing screening to nonlinear pricing design.

Mussa-Rosen and Maskin-Riley derive optimal screening menus under single-crossing.

Where it is used

Insurance, second-degree price discrimination, optimal income taxation (Mirrlees), and credit rationing.

Dual of signaling — the uninformed party moves first.
