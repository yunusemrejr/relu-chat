Common Knowledge

An event is common knowledge if everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on ad infinitum.

Definition

Formally EEE is common knowledge if E⊆⋂n≥1KnEE\subseteq\bigcap_{n\ge 1}K^n EE⊆⋂n≥1​KnE, where KE=⋂iKiEK E=\bigcap_i K_i EKE=⋂i​Ki​E and KiK_iKi​ denotes player iii's knowledge operator.

In a Kripke model, EEE is common knowledge at state ω\omegaω iff EEE holds at every state reachable by the reflexive transitive closure of the agents' accessibility relations.

Intuition

It is the infinite tower of mutual knowledge about a fact — not just you know, but you know I know, and I know you know I know, …

Small failures of this tower can dramatically change strategic outcomes.

Worked example

Aumann's "Agreeing to Disagree": common priors plus common knowledge of posteriors implies equal posteriors.

The Muddy Children / Blue-Eyed Islanders puzzle hinges on common knowledge of the puzzle setup.

The math

Rubinstein's e-mail game shows that approximate common knowledge is strictly weaker than full common knowledge.

Monderer-Samet characterize common knowledge via self-evident events.

Where it is used

Foundational for epistemic game theory, speculative trade, and cryptographic coordination.

Critical in the analysis of market microstructure and information cascades.
