Proper Equilibrium

A refinement of trembling-hand perfection where more costly mistakes are infinitely less likely.

Definition

Proper equilibria are always trembling-hand perfect and are invariant under elimination of strictly dominated strategies.

Intuition

Players are infinitely more careful about costly errors: a mistake that hurts more is made with vanishingly smaller probability.

Myerson (1978) introduced the concept to address the limitations of trembling-hand perfection.

Worked example

In any normal-form game, proper equilibrium refines trembling-hand perfect by ruling out equilibria that rely on certain domination relationships.

A proper equilibrium always exists in every finite normal-form game (Myerson).

The math

Every proper equilibrium is trembling-hand perfect, and every trembling-hand perfect equilibrium is proper in generic games.

Van Damme (1984): proper equilibria are characterized by a lexicographic hierarchy of caution.

Where it is used

Refined equilibrium selection in signaling and bargaining games.

Used in mechanism design to eliminate fragile Nash equilibria.
