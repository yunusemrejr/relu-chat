Mean Field Game

A game with a continuum of players where each player's influence on aggregate is negligible, simplifying equilibrium to a PDE system.

Definition

In a mean-field game, the state of the game is the distribution mtm_tmt​ of agents over states. Each agent solves a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation ∂tu+H(x,∇u,m)=0\partial_t u + H(x,\nabla u, m) = 0∂t​u+H(x,∇u,m)=0, and the population evolves via the Fokker-Planck equation ∂tm−div(m ∂pH)=0\partial_t m - \mathrm{div}(m\,\partial_p H) = 0∂t​m−div(m∂p​H)=0. The system (u,m)(u,m)(u,m) forms the MFG equilibrium.

Lasry-Lions (2006) and Huang-Caines-Malhamé (2006) independently developed the theory.

Intuition

With millions of agents, no individual can affect aggregate — but all are affected by it. The equilibrium couples optimal control (forward by agents) and population dynamics (forward in time).

MFG reduces a massive interactive system to two coupled PDEs, making analysis tractable.

Worked example

Taxi fleets: each driver chooses a location; the distribution of all drivers determines earnings. The MFG equilibrium captures spatial equilibrium.

Emission models: each firm decides abatement; aggregate emissions determine climate damage.

The math

A stationary MFG: λ+H(x,∇u,m)=0\lambda + H(x,\nabla u, m) = 0λ+H(x,∇u,m)=0 and div(m ∂pH)=0\mathrm{div}(m\,\partial_p H)=0div(m∂p​H)=0, solved together with boundary conditions.

Existence and uniqueness of classical solutions require monotonicity of the Hamiltonian in mmm (Lasry-Lions).

Where it is used

Economics (labor markets, wealth distribution), finance (systemic risk), engineering (autonomous driving), and epidemiology.

Active frontier in applied mathematics and algorithmic game theory.
