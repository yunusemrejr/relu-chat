Bertrand Competition

Simultaneous price-setting competition among firms producing a homogeneous good.

Ask the Game theory assistant1 min read · Updated September 9, 2026

Definition

Firms simultaneously post prices pip_ipi​; demand goes entirely to the lowest price (split if tied). Profit is $\pi_i=(p_i-c)D(p_i)\cdot \mathbf 1\{p_i\le p_{-i}\}\cdot\text{share}.

The "Bertrand paradox" arises with identical goods and marginal cost ccc: the unique Nash equilibrium is pi=cp_i=cpi​=c for all iii.

Intuition

Price undercutting drives price to marginal cost with even as few as two firms.

Tiny asymmetries in cost or differentiation change the picture dramatically.

Worked example

Two symmetric firms with constant marginal cost ccc both price at ccc in equilibrium, earning zero profit.

Differentiated Bertrand with logit demand yields positive equilibrium markups.

The math

With capacity constraints (Edgeworth-Bertrand) or product differentiation, equilibrium prices exceed marginal cost.

Kreps-Scheinkman: Cournot outcomes emerge as the subgame perfect equilibrium of a two-stage capacity-then-price game.

Where it is used

Explains why concentrated markets still exhibit competitive pricing if products are homogeneous.

Motivates product differentiation as a profit-protection strategy.
