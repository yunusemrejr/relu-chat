A mixed Nash equilibrium requires each player to be indifferent among all pure strategies in their support.

Intuition

Randomization is optimal when predictability is exploitable — it keeps opponents guessing.

A mixing player must be indifferent among the pure strategies in the support, otherwise they'd collapse to a pure best response.

Worked example

The math

Nash's existence theorem guarantees a mixed equilibrium in every finite game. Harsanyi (1973) showed mixing is the limit of slightly perturbed pure-strategy play (purification).

Used in security games, poker bluffing, penalty-kick analysis, and randomized algorithms.

Essential for analyzing games lacking pure equilibria.
