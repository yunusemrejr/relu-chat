Signaling Games

Dynamic incomplete-information games where an informed sender chooses a signal that a receiver observes.

Definition

Sender of type θ∈Θ\theta\in\Thetaθ∈Θ chooses signal m∈Mm\in Mm∈M; receiver observes mmm and chooses action a∈Aa\in Aa∈A. Payoffs uS(m,a,θ)u_S(m,a,\theta)uS​(m,a,θ), uR(m,a,θ)u_R(m,a,\theta)uR​(m,a,θ); prior p(θ)p(\theta)p(θ).

Solution concepts: separating, pooling, and semi-separating perfect Bayesian equilibria.

Intuition

The informed party conveys information through costly action choices.

Equilibria are sustained by receiver beliefs that make deviation unprofitable for the sender.

Worked example

Spence (1973): education signals worker ability; separating equilibria have high-ability workers study more than low-ability workers would.

Costly signaling in biology (peacock tails) — the handicap principle.

The math

Intuitive criterion (Cho-Kreps) refines PBE by restricting off-path beliefs.

Single-crossing / Spence-Mirrlees condition guarantees a unique separating equilibrium.

Where it is used

Job-market signaling, advertising, political posturing, and warranty design.

Central in information economics and biology.

Related topics

  • Perfect Bayesian EquilibriumA refinement for dynamic games of incomplete information pairing strategies with beliefs.
  • Bayesian GameA game in which players have private information (types) drawn from a common prior.
  • Screening GamesGames where an uninformed principal offers a menu of contracts to sort informed agents.
  • Principal-Agent ProblemA contracting problem where an agent's action is unobservable to the principal.
  • Sequential EquilibriumA refinement of PBE requiring beliefs to be limits of fully mixed strategy perturbations.
  • Cheap TalkCostless, non-binding, and verifiable communication prior to play that can influence equilibrium selection.
