Ultimatum Game

A two-stage bargaining game highlighting the gap between rational prediction and observed fairness.

Definition

Proposer offers a split (x,1−x)(x,1-x)(x,1−x) of a pie; responder accepts (payoffs (x,1−x)(x,1-x)(x,1−x)) or rejects (payoffs (0,0)(0,0)(0,0)).

Subgame-perfect equilibrium: proposer offers x→1x\to 1x→1, responder accepts any x<1x<1x<1.

Intuition

Self-interested rationality predicts a near-zero offer accepted; experiments show offers of 404040-505050% and rejection of small offers.

Fairness, reciprocity, and spite enter the empirical picture.

Worked example

In the classical G\"uth-Schmittberger-Schwarze (1982) experiment, modal offers are 50/5050/5050/50 and offers below 202020% are routinely rejected.

Dictator-game variants remove responder power to isolate pure altruism.

The math

SPE predictions fail empirically — led to behavioral economics models such as inequity aversion (Fehr-Schmidt) and reciprocity (Rabin).

Evolutionary models with reputational tracking recover near-fair offers.

Where it is used

Behavioral economics, experimental game theory, and cross-cultural anthropology (Henrich et al.).

Foundational data point for bounded rationality research.
