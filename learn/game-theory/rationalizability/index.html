Rationalizability

The set of strategies surviving iterated elimination of strategies that are never a best response.

Ask the Game theory assistant →1 min read · Updated September 9, 2026

Definition

A strategy sis_isi​ is rationalizable if it survives iterated deletion of never-best-responses, i.e., it is a best response to some belief over the surviving opponent strategies.

Formally the largest fixed point of the best-response operator.

Intuition

A strategy is rationalizable if you can construct a chain of consistent beliefs that makes it optimal.

Weaker than Nash equilibrium — imposes common knowledge of rationality but not of beliefs.

Worked example

In a 2×22\times 22×2 game with a strictly dominated action, only the dominant strategy is rationalizable.

In zero-sum games, rationalizable strategies coincide with minimax strategies.

The math

Bernheim and Pearce (1984): rationalizability is the common-knowledge-of-rationality solution concept.

In two-player games, rationalizability equals iterated strict dominance.

Where it is used

Useful for analyzing games where beliefs about equilibrium play are hard to justify.

Foundation of epistemic game theory.
